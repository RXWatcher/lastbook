# Begin making your emergency plan today
Emergencies are difficult to predict. So it’s best to think ahead. Making an emergency plan is one way to do this. It contains clear agreements with the people around you on what you will do in an emergency situation. For example, how you will reach or locate each other, plus arrangements for anyone requiring extra help.

## Follow these steps to make an emergency plan
• 1. Fill in the questions with your household.
• 2. Discuss the emergency plan with other people. For example, your children, parents or neighbours.
• 3. Take a photo or make a copy of your emergency plan and share it with the emergency contacts you’ve filled in.
• 4. Print it out and keep it in a handy place. For example, in your emergency kit, next to your meter cupboard or in your hall cupboard.
• 5. Update your emergency plan regularly, so it’s always up-to-date.

## 7 Important agreements
Think about situations such as:
• You’re at work and can’t get home. Your phone isn’t working. Who will collect the children from school?
• You have mobility issues and the lift in your apartment block isn’t working. How can you get outside?
• You can’t reach your partner by phone. Where are you going to meet?
• You are dependent on home care or medical equipment. How will you get the care you need?
• You live in a student house and your family live in another city. How can you stay in contact?
• The internet is down and you are looking for information during an emergency situation. Where can you find reliable information?

Once you’ve made your emergency plan, discuss it with your partner, children, parents and/or neighbours. Take a photo or make a copy of your emergency plan and share it with the emergency contacts you’ve filled in. These are the people you can go to if something happens.

Keep the emergency plan in a handy place. For example with your emergency kit, on the fridge, in the meter cupboard or in the hall cupboard.
